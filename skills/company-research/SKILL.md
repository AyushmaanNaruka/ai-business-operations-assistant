# Company research

Produce a structured profile, not a pile of pages.

## The profile to fill

| Field | Usually found on |
|---|---|
| What they do, in one sentence | Homepage hero, meta description |
| Who they sell to | Homepage, customers page, case studies |
| Positioning and claimed differentiator | Homepage, about, product pages |
| Products or plans | Product page, pricing page |
| Pricing signals | Pricing page. Record "contact sales" as a signal, it means enterprise motion |
| Recent activity | Blog, news, changelog |
| Scale signals | Careers page (open roles and their seniority), customer logos |

## Method

1. Search for the company to find the canonical domain. Do not assume it.
2. Read the homepage first. It sets vocabulary for everything else.
3. Read pricing before product. Pricing tells you who they actually sell to more reliably than any about page.
4. Stop at the page cap. Depth past five or six pages rarely changes the profile.

## Rules

**Every claim carries its URL and the date it was read.** Web claims go stale in a way an uploaded file does not, and the reader needs to know when you looked.

**A company's own website is a marketing document.** It states positioning, not fact. When the profile says "the leading platform for X", record it as a claim they make, not as a property they have.

**Empty fields stay empty.** If pricing is not published, the profile says pricing is not published. It does not estimate.

**If search quota is exhausted, stop and report it.** Do not answer from training data. A profile assembled from memory is indistinguishable from a fabricated one, which makes it worse than no profile.

## Output

A structured profile with one line per field, each carrying its citation, followed by an explicit list of what could not be determined.
