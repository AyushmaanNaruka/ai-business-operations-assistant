# Campaign report

Inherits `evidence-citation`.

## Structure

1. **Executive summary** first, always. Half a page. The three findings and the recommendation. Written so someone who reads nothing else is correctly informed
2. **What we looked at** sources, date range, and any data quality caveats
3. **Findings** ordered by business impact, not chronology. One subsection each, with the number, the evidence, and what it means
4. **What is not working** a real section, not a euphemism buried in findings
5. **Recommendations** each tied to findings, each with an expected effect and a way to measure it
6. **Method** how the numbers were computed, briefly
7. **Sources** every evidence entry

## Rules

**The executive summary is written last and read first.** It is not an introduction. It contains conclusions.

**Every finding has a "so what".** A number without an implication is a statistic, not a finding.

**No recommendation without a supporting figure.** If it rests on judgment rather than data, say so explicitly and mark it as such.

**Caveats go inline, not in a footnote.** If a finding rests on two months of data, that belongs in the sentence, not at the bottom of the page.

## Tone

Direct. Short sentences. Active voice. No throat clearing. A business reader's time is the scarcest input.

Write "Paid social spend rose 60 percent since June while revenue stayed flat" not "It is worth noting that there appears to have been some increase in paid social investment which may not have been matched by corresponding revenue growth."

## Length

Four to eight pages for a quarterly review. If it runs longer, the appendix is doing work the body should not.
