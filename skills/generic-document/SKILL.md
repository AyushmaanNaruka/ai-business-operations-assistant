# Generic business document

Inherits `evidence-citation`.

The brief asks for "other appropriate business outputs". This skill exists so an unusual request produces a well structured document rather than a refusal.

Use this for things like a competitor battlecard, a meeting brief, a QBR agenda, a positioning one pager, a stakeholder update.

## Method

1. **Name what the document is for.** Who reads it, when, and what they do afterwards. Write that down before writing anything else; it determines the structure.
2. **Pick a structure that fits that job**, from these patterns:

| Job | Pattern |
|---|---|
| Inform a decision | Situation, options, recommendation, risks |
| Prepare someone for a conversation | Context, what they will say, what we say, proof points |
| Compare things | One row per option, the same columns for all of them |
| Track and update | Status, what changed, what is blocked, what is next |
| Enable someone to act | Steps in order, each with an owner |

3. **Apply the house rules.** Executive summary first if it runs beyond two pages. Every number cites evidence. Gaps stated explicitly. Conclusions cite findings.
4. **State the structure you chose and why** in one line at the top of the artifact plan, so the choice is reviewable.

## Rules

**Never refuse for being unlisted.** If the request is a business document, this skill covers it.

**Do refuse, clearly, when it is not a document at all.** A request to send an email, update a CRM, or schedule a meeting is unsupported. Say what cannot be done and what can.

**Do not pad.** A battlecard is one page. An update is half a page. Length should come from content, never from a template.
