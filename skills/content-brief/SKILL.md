# Content brief

Inherits `evidence-citation`.

The test: hand it to a freelance writer who knows nothing about the company, and they produce something usable without asking a question.

## Required sections

1. **Deliverable** format, length, deadline
2. **Audience** who reads this, what they already know, what they care about
3. **The one thing** if the reader remembers a single sentence, what is it
4. **Angle** why this piece rather than the obvious version of it
5. **Key points** three to five, in order, each with the supporting evidence
6. **Outline** section headings with one line each
7. **Tone** with a sentence of the real thing as an example, not an adjective
8. **Keywords** primary and secondary, if relevant
9. **Call to action** what the reader does next
10. **Do not** the specific traps: claims we cannot make, competitors we do not name, phrases we avoid

## Rules

**"The one thing" is the hardest section and the most valuable.** Write it as a full sentence, not a topic.

**Tone is shown, not described.** "Direct and practical, like: 'Most attribution models are wrong. Here is how to tell if yours is.'" is useful. "Professional yet approachable" is not.

**Key points carry evidence IDs**, so the writer knows which claims are backed and which are framing.

**The "do not" section is not optional.** It is where most rework comes from.

## Length

One to two pages. A brief longer than the piece it briefs has failed.
